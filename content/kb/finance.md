# Finance options explained

Harbourline can arrange finance through Toyota Finance and other lenders. This article explains the common products in plain language. **All figures are examples only.** They are not a quote, an offer of credit or financial advice. Rates, fees and approval depend on the lender's assessment of your circumstances.

## Interest rate vs comparison rate

- The **interest rate** is what the lender charges on the balance.
- The **comparison rate** combines the interest rate with most fees and charges into a single annual figure, so you can compare loans fairly. Australian law requires lenders to show it. It is usually calculated on a standard example (for example a $30,000 secured loan over 5 years), so for other amounts and terms the true cost may differ.
- Promotional "low rate" offers sometimes come with a higher vehicle price or fewer discounts. Always compare the total amount payable.

## Standard car loan (consumer loan)

You borrow the amount financed and repay it in equal monthly instalments, owning the car at the end.

**Example only:** $50,000 financed over 60 months at 7.49% p.a., no balloon, no fees included: about **$1,002 a month**, total repayments about $60,100.

## Loan with a balloon (residual)

A balloon is a lump sum left owing at the end of the term. It lowers monthly repayments but you must pay, refinance or sell the car to clear it.

**Example only:** $50,000 financed over 60 months at 7.49% p.a. with a $15,000 (30%) balloon: about **$795 a month**, then $15,000 at the end. Total payable about $62,700, more than the no-balloon example because you carry more debt for longer.

## Guaranteed Future Value (GFV)

A GFV product (offered by Toyota Finance under its own product name) sets a guaranteed minimum value for the car at the end of a 2-4 year term, based on agreed kilometres and condition. At the end you can:

1. **Trade in** the car at Harbourline and use any equity above the GFV towards your next Toyota;
2. **Return** the car (subject to fair wear and tear and kilometre limits) and walk away from the final payment; or
3. **Keep** the car by paying or refinancing the final payment.

**Example only:** $60,000 vehicle, $10,000 deposit, $50,000 financed over 36 months at 7.49% p.a. with a GFV of $27,000: about **$884 a month**. Excess kilometres and damage charges apply if you return the car.

GFV suits people who like to change cars every few years and want certainty about the end value.

## Novated lease

A novated lease is a three-way agreement between you, your employer and a lease company. Lease payments and running costs (fuel or charging, servicing, tyres, registration, insurance) are deducted from your salary, partly before tax.

- **Petrol, hybrid and diesel vehicles:** you normally make a post-tax contribution to reduce fringe benefits tax (FBT); the benefit comes mainly from GST savings and bundled running costs.
- **Electric vehicles:** eligible battery-electric cars under the luxury car tax threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles are currently exempt from FBT, which can make a bZ4X much cheaper to drive on a novated lease. Plug-in hybrids lost this exemption from 1 April 2025 (existing arrangements excepted).
- A residual (set by the ATO's minimum schedule) is payable at the end.

Ask your employer whether they offer salary packaging and speak to a tax adviser about your situation.

## Business finance

ABN holders can use a chattel mortgage, where the business owns the vehicle from day one and may claim GST, depreciation and interest (subject to the business-use percentage). Talk to your accountant.

## Deposits and trade-ins

A trade-in can serve as all or part of your deposit. We value trade-ins on the spot, and can pay out existing finance on your trade.

## Responsible lending

We will ask about your income, expenses and existing debts because lenders are required to check that a loan is suitable. Never commit to repayments you are not comfortable with.
