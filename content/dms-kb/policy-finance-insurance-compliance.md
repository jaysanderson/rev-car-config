# Finance and insurance compliance

**Policy owner:** Business Manager (F&I) and General Manager · **Applies to:** every conversation, quote and document about finance, insurance or protection products · **Effective:** 1 July 2026

## Purpose

Harbourline arranges finance and sells insurance as a credit representative and an authorised representative of our licensed partners. This policy sets out what sales staff and business managers may and may not do so that customers are treated fairly and Harbourline stays within its authorisations.

This policy is a plain-English summary for staff. It does not replace training, the partners' licence conditions or legal advice.

## Who can do what

| Activity | Salesperson | Business manager (F&I) |
|---|---|---|
| Say that finance is available and name our lenders | Yes | Yes |
| Show an example repayment from a published lender rate, clearly labelled as an example | Yes | Yes |
| Collect a finance enquiry and pass it to F&I | Yes | Yes |
| Ask about income, expenses, debts, or make credit assessments | No | Yes |
| Submit a finance application | No | Yes |
| Recommend a specific loan, term, balloon or product | No | Only within their credit representative authorisation and after the required inquiries |
| Discuss or sell insurance or protection products | General information only | Yes, within authorisation |

Salespeople refer every finance and insurance question beyond general information to the business manager.

## Responsible lending basics

Before a credit contract is arranged, the business manager must:

1. **Make reasonable inquiries** about the customer's requirements and objectives (what they need the loan for, the term they want, whether they want a balloon) and their financial situation (income, regular expenses, existing debts).
2. **Take reasonable steps to verify** that information, for example payslips, bank statements or tax returns.
3. **Assess whether the loan is not unsuitable**: the customer can meet repayments without substantial hardship and the loan meets their requirements.
4. **Record** the inquiries, verification and assessment on the finance application. The customer may ask for a copy of the assessment.

A balloon or guaranteed future value must be explained, including that the customer must pay or refinance the final amount at the end of the term.

## Before submitting a finance application

The business manager may submit an application only when the deal file holds:

1. A quote at stage **negotiation** or later with the agreed drive-away price and any required discount approval.
2. Identity: driver licence sighted and recorded (a licence copy may be uploaded for the lender's identity check).
3. The customer's requirements and objectives, and income, expense and debt details, with verification documents (two recent payslips or the last tax return for self-employed, and recent bank statements).
4. **If there is a trade-in:** the verified trade-in appraisal form (kind **trade_in_form**), the PPSR certificate, and the payout letter from the trade-in's lender if finance is owing, so the amount financed reflects the trade-in allowance less any payout. Negative equity must be disclosed to the lender.
5. The customer's signed privacy and credit-check consent.

## No advice beyond licence

- Never tell a customer which finance or insurance product is "best for them" unless you are authorised to give that advice and have done what the authorisation requires.
- Never say or imply that the customer must take dealer finance or insurance to get the car, the price, a discount or a promotion. Concessions are approved on front gross only.
- Never make statements about a customer's eligibility for credit before the lender decides.
- Never complete or alter any part of an application on the customer's behalf without their instruction, and never change income or expense figures.

## Disclosure

- Every finance quote shows the **interest rate**, the **comparison rate** and its warning, fees, the term, any balloon and the **total amount payable**.
- The customer receives the credit guide and the lender's documents before signing.
- The customer is told that Harbourline may receive a commission from the lender or insurer and, if asked, how much.
- Add-on insurance and protection products may only be offered after the customer has agreed to buy the vehicle, must be explained as optional, and the customer's decision must be recorded. Premium, cover, exclusions and the cooling-off period are disclosed in writing.
- Promotional finance rates must be shown with any conditions (for example, a lower rate that is not available with a discount).

## What the AI assistant may do

The DMS assistant and the follow-up drafts may describe finance products in general terms, quote published example rates labelled as examples, and suggest that the customer speak to the business manager. They must not recommend a product, assess suitability or promise approval. Drafts that do should be corrected before sending.

## Complaints and hardship

A customer who complains about finance or insurance, or says they are having trouble making repayments, is referred to the business manager the same day and given the lender's hardship and complaints contacts.

## Records

Finance applications, assessments, disclosure acknowledgements and insurance decisions are kept for seven years. Status changes are recorded in the DMS finance application (submitted, approved, conditional, declined, settled).

## Breaches

Report any suspected breach to the general manager immediately. Do not try to fix a completed application yourself.
